## Why a news vocabulary helps

News is repetitive in a useful way. The same words for government, the economy and public life appear day after day. Learn a few dozen, and a headline that looked impenetrable becomes readable. This is a fast route into French news, particularly at B1 and above.

## Headline patterns

French headlines often use noun phrases instead of full sentences. Here are the patterns you will see most.

| Pattern | Example | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *hausse / baisse de* + noun | *Hausse des prix en septembre* | Prices rise in September |
| noun + *en* + direction | *Le chômage en baisse* | Unemployment falling |
| *grève* + place or sector | *Grève dans les transports* | Strike in transport |
| noun + *prévu(e)* + time | *Réforme prévue en janvier* | Reform planned for January |
| quotation or question | *Que va changer la réforme ?* | What will the reform change? |

Notice how few verbs there are. The meaning sits in the nouns, which is why learning them pays off.

## A worked set of headlines

These headlines are original, written for this guide. Read each and say what is happening.

1. *Hausse des prix de l'énergie : le gouvernement annonce de nouvelles mesures.*
2. *Grève dans les transports jeudi, les syndicats appellent à manifester.*
3. *Le chômage en légère baisse au troisième trimestre.*

**Answers:**

1. Energy prices are rising and the government announces new measures.
2. There is a transport strike on Thursday, and the unions call for demonstrations.
3. Unemployment fell slightly in the third quarter.

## How to build the habit

1. **Read three headlines a day** on a topic you follow.
2. **Pick out the nouns** before you look at any verb.
3. **Note repeated words.** If a word appears three times in a week, learn it.
4. **Move to the first paragraph** when a headline interests you.
