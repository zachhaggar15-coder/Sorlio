## Why people look for an alternative

Duolingo is a good place to start learning French. It gives you a habit, a sense of progress and an easy first few hundred words. Many learners who later look for alternatives did exactly that, and they often feel they gained something real from it.

The question usually arises at a particular point. A learner finishes the early units, finds that the exercises repeat in similar patterns, and notices that they can complete a lesson without being able to read a paragraph in a newspaper. Others want to read a novel, follow a French podcast or handle a real conversation, and the short exercise format does not give them that practice. Those are reasonable reasons to look elsewhere.

It is worth being clear about what is happening here. The problem is usually not that Duolingo is ineffective for what it is designed to do. It is that a learner's goal has moved beyond what that format covers.

## Recognition is not comprehension

Much of what short exercises train is recognition. You see a word, you match it to a meaning or pick the right answer from a set. That skill is useful, and it is measurable within a lesson. Reading comprehension is different. It means working out a sentence you have never seen, using the words you know, the grammar you have absorbed and the context around the sentence.

Researchers have long distinguished between learning words in isolation and meeting them repeatedly in running text. Reading exposes you to the same words in different sentences, which helps you recognise them when the context changes. It also shows you how French builds sentences over several lines, something a single exercise rarely does.

A simple test helps. Pick a short paragraph from a French news article written for adults. Can you say roughly what it is about, and identify the key verbs? If not, your recognition skills may be ahead of your comprehension, and reading practice is the next step.

## Structured exercises versus longer reading

Both approaches have value, and they train different things.

**Structured exercises** are good at:
- giving you a daily routine that is easy to keep
- introducing a controlled set of words and patterns
- showing you immediately whether you got something right

**Longer reading** is good at:
- exposing you to natural sentence structure and idiom
- letting you meet familiar words in new contexts
- building the reading stamina you need for books and articles

The weakness of exercises is that they can become a closed loop, where you get better at the exercises rather than at French. The weakness of reading is that it can be frustrating if the text is too hard. The fix is to choose reading that is slightly below your level, where most words are familiar, and to look up the rest.

## The strengths of alternative approaches

There is no single alternative to Duolingo. The main categories are:

- **Graded-reader and story apps.** These give you stories at set levels, often with audio. They suit people who want to read a full text from the start.
- **Reading tools for your own material.** These let you read articles, books or pages you choose, and save the words you need. They suit intermediate learners.
- **Structured courses.** These explain grammar in depth and follow a syllabus. They suit people who want to understand why a structure works.
- **Speaking tools and tutors.** These focus on output and pronunciation, which reading apps do not cover.

Sorlio sits in the reading category. It is an Android app for reading French stories and news, with tap-to-look-up vocabulary in context, saved words for review and listening while you read. For a learner who has outgrown lesson-style exercises and wants more reading, it is an appealing next step. It is not a replacement for a course, and it does not teach speaking.

## When continuing with Duolingo makes sense

Staying with Duolingo is a perfectly good choice in several situations:

- You are still at the very beginning and want a daily habit before anything else.
- You enjoy the format and it keeps you studying, which matters more than any method.
- You are using it alongside a course, a tutor or reading, as one part of a wider routine.
- Your goal is a modest amount of basic French for a trip, and the lessons cover what you need.

The reasonable test is not "is Duolingo the best?" but "is it still moving me toward what I want?" If you can answer yes, keep going. If the honest answer is no, look for an alternative that matches the gap.

## Using Duolingo and reading together

Many learners get the best results from combining the two. Use the short exercises to keep a daily habit, and add ten to fifteen minutes of reading a few times a week. Over time you can shift the balance as your reading improves. The shift itself is the sign that you are progressing.
