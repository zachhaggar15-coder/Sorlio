## Why two past tenses?

English can say "I walked" and "I was walking", but it often uses the same simple past for both a single event and a habit. French separates them more clearly. It uses the *passé composé* for things that happened, and the *imparfait* for the situation around them. Once you can feel that difference, French stories become much easier to follow, because the tenses tell you whether you are reading about the plot or the background.

## The core idea

Think of a story as a stage.

- The **imparfait** sets the stage: the weather, how people felt, what was going on, what used to happen.
- The **passé composé** moves the plot: the events that happened, one after another.

Here is a short example:

> *Il était huit heures. Il faisait froid et la ville dormait encore. Marc a ouvert la porte, il a regardé la rue, puis il est parti.*

*Il était huit heures*, *il faisait froid* and *la ville dormait* describe the scene, so they are in the imparfait. *Marc a ouvert*, *il a regardé* and *il est parti* are three events in sequence, so they are in the passé composé. If you remove the first two sentences, the story still moves. If you remove the last, nothing happens.

## Clue words

Certain words push you toward one tense.

| Usually points to the imparfait | Usually points to the passé composé |
| --- | --- |
| *d'habitude*, *souvent*, *toujours* | *hier*, *ce matin*, *un jour* |
| *chaque jour*, *tous les étés* | *soudain*, *tout à coup* |
| *pendant que*, *à cette époque* | *d'abord*, *puis*, *ensuite* |
| *quand j'étais petit* | *une fois*, *deux fois* |

These are guides, not rules. A sentence with *souvent* is in the imparfait because the action is a repeated habit, not because of the word itself.

## How the forms look

You will recognise them quickly with a little practice.

- **Passé composé:** an auxiliary (*avoir* or *être*) plus a past participle: *j'ai mangé*, *elle est partie*, *nous avons fini*.
- **Imparfait:** one word with an ending in *-ais*, *-ait*, *-ions*, *-iez* or *-aient*: *je mangeais*, *elle partait*, *nous finissions*.

The imparfait ending *-ait* is the one you will see most in stories, since narration is mostly third person: *il y avait*, *elle voulait*, *c'était*.

## Both tenses in one sentence

The classic pattern is an ongoing situation interrupted by an event:

> *Je lisais dans le jardin quand le téléphone a sonné.*
> I was reading in the garden when the phone rang.

*Je lisais* is the background, and *a sonné* is the event that cut through it. Many stories are built from this pattern: *Elle dormait quand quelqu'un a frappé à la porte.*

## Same verb, different meaning

Some verbs change meaning with the tense. This is worth noticing when reading.

- *Je savais la réponse.* (I knew the answer, a state.)
- *J'ai su la réponse hier.* (I found out the answer yesterday, an event.)

The first describes what was in your head. The second reports the moment you learned it.

## A worked passage

Read this one slowly and sort each verb into "stage" or "plot".

> *Quand j'étais enfant, nous passions l'été chez ma grand-mère. Chaque matin, elle préparait du pain. Un jour, j'ai trouvé un vieux livre dans le grenier, et je l'ai lu en entier.*

- *étais*, *passions*, *préparait*: imparfait, the repeated background of the summers.
- *ai trouvé*, *ai lu*: passé composé, a single day's event.

The sentence moves from a habitual past to one particular day, which is exactly the shift the two tenses mark.

## A short exercise

Choose the form that fits, then check the answers below.

1. Quand il (arriver) ____, il (pleuvoir) ____.
2. Chaque été, nous (aller) ____ à la mer.
3. Hier, elle (finir) ____ son travail à six heures.
4. Il (dormir) ____ quand le téléphone (sonner) ____.
5. Je (savoir) ____ qu'il était malade depuis longtemps.

**Answers:**

1. *Quand il est arrivé, il pleuvait.* The arrival is an event, the rain is the scene.
2. *Chaque été, nous allions à la mer.* *Chaque* signals a habit.
3. *Hier, elle a fini son travail à six heures.* A completed event with a specific time.
4. *Il dormait quand le téléphone a sonné.* The ongoing state is interrupted by an event.
5. *Je savais qu'il était malade depuis longtemps.* A state of knowledge in the past.

## How to practise while reading

When you read a French story, underline the verbs of one paragraph and label each one "stage" or "plot". After a few paragraphs the sorting becomes automatic, and you will notice that the writer moves between the two tenses to control the pace.
