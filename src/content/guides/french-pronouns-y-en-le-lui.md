## The problem for readers

Small words cause big confusion. A sentence like *Elle y pense souvent* contains no noun at all for the thing she thinks about. You have to work out what *y* stands for by looking back. French uses these short pronouns constantly, and each one is a pointer to something that appeared earlier.

This guide gives you a way to decode them.

## The six that matter

| Pronoun | Replaces | Rough meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *le*, *la*, *l'* | a direct object (singular) | him, her, it | *Je le vois.* (I see him/it.) |
| *les* | a direct object (plural) | them | *Je les vois.* (I see them.) |
| *lui* | à + a person (singular) | to him, to her | *Je lui parle.* (I speak to him/her.) |
| *leur* | à + people (plural) | to them | *Je leur parle.* (I speak to them.) |
| *y* | à + a thing or a place | there, to it | *J'y vais.* (I am going there.) |
| *en* | de + a thing, or a quantity | of it, some | *J'en veux.* (I want some.) |

Notice that *lui* and *leur* are for people and *y* is for things and places. That is the biggest single clue.

## Where they sit

The pronoun goes directly before the verb that governs it.

- Present: *Je le vois*, *Elle lui parle*, *Nous y allons*.
- Compound tense: before the auxiliary: *Je l'ai vu*, *Elle lui a parlé*, *Nous y sommes allés*.
- Infinitive: before the infinitive: *Je vais le voir*, *Elle veut lui parler*.
- Negative: the *ne* goes before the pronoun and *pas* after the verb: *Je ne le vois pas*, *Je n'en veux pas*.

## A decoding routine

When you hit a pronoun you do not understand:

1. **Find the verb it belongs to.**
2. **Ask: what does this verb take?** If it takes à + a person, expect *lui* or *leur*. If it takes à + a thing, expect *y*. If it takes *de* + a thing, expect *en*.
3. **Look back** one or two sentences for the noun that fits.
4. **Substitute** the noun in your head and reread.

Here is that routine at work:

> *Marie a trouvé un vieux livre dans le grenier. Elle l'a lu en une nuit, et elle en a parlé à ses amis.*

- *l'* in *Elle l'a lu* stands for *un vieux livre*, a direct object.
- *en* in *elle en a parlé* stands for "about it" (*parler de quelque chose*), so *le livre*.

Substituted: "She read the book in one night, and she talked about it to her friends."

## Two pronouns that confuse learners

**Y is not only "there".** *Je pense à mon examen* becomes *J'y pense*. Here *y* replaces *à mon examen*, a thing, not a place.

**En often means "some" or "any".** *Tu veux du pain ? Oui, j'en veux.* The *en* carries *du pain*. In a negative, *Je n'en veux pas* means "I don't want any".

## A short passage

Read it and find what each pronoun points to.

> *Léa avait une sœur, Camille. Elle lui a écrit une lettre et elle l'a envoyée le lendemain. Camille n'y a pas répondu tout de suite, parce qu'elle avait beaucoup de choses à faire.*

**Answers:**

- *lui*: *à Camille* (to Camille).
- *l'* (in *l'a envoyée*): *la lettre*.
- *y* (in *n'y a pas répondu*): *à la lettre* (to the letter).

## Quick practice

Rewrite each sentence with a pronoun in place of the underlined idea, then check.

1. *Je donne le livre à mon frère.* → *Je le lui donne.* (direct *le* and indirect *lui*)
2. *Nous allons à Paris.* → *Nous y allons.*
3. *Elle mange des pommes.* → *Elle en mange.*
4. *J'écris à mes amis.* → *Je leur écris.*
