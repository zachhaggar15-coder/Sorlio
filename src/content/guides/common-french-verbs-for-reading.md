## Why start with verbs?

A French sentence hangs on its verb. If you can recognise the verb and its subject, you already have the skeleton of the meaning, and the nouns fill in the rest. A small group of verbs appears so often that knowing them well changes how much of a page you can read.

The list below is not scientific. It collects verbs that show up constantly in everyday stories and news. Learn them in their forms as well as their infinitives, because you will meet them conjugated.

## The twenty

| Verb | Main meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *être* | to be | *Elle est fatiguée.* (She is tired.) |
| *avoir* | to have | *Nous avons un chien.* (We have a dog.) |
| *aller* | to go | *Je vais au marché.* (I'm going to the market.) |
| *faire* | to do, to make | *Il fait un gâteau.* (He is making a cake.) |
| *dire* | to say | *Elle dit la vérité.* (She tells the truth.) |
| *pouvoir* | to be able to, can | *Je peux venir demain.* (I can come tomorrow.) |
| *vouloir* | to want | *Ils veulent partir.* (They want to leave.) |
| *savoir* | to know (a fact), to know how | *Je sais nager.* (I know how to swim.) |
| *voir* | to see | *Nous voyons la mer.* (We see the sea.) |
| *devoir* | to have to, must | *Tu dois dormir.* (You must sleep.) |
| *venir* | to come | *Elle vient de Paris.* (She comes from Paris.) |
| *prendre* | to take | *Il prend le train.* (He takes the train.) |
| *donner* | to give | *Elle donne un cadeau.* (She gives a present.) |
| *mettre* | to put | *Je mets les clés sur la table.* (I put the keys on the table.) |
| *trouver* | to find | *Il trouve un vieux livre.* (He finds an old book.) |
| *parler* | to speak | *Nous parlons français.* (We speak French.) |
| *penser* | to think | *Je pense à toi.* (I'm thinking of you.) |
| *demander* | to ask | *Elle demande son chemin.* (She asks the way.) |
| *rester* | to stay | *Il reste à la maison.* (He stays at home.) |
| *arriver* | to arrive, to happen | *Le train arrive à midi.* (The train arrives at noon.) |

## How they work in a sentence

Three of these do double duty, which is why they matter so much.

- **Être and avoir** build the passé composé: *il est parti*, *nous avons mangé*. When you see them followed by a past participle, you are reading a past tense.
- **Aller** builds the near future: *je vais partir* (I'm going to leave).
- **Pouvoir, vouloir, devoir, savoir** usually take an infinitive straight after: *elle veut sortir*, *il doit travailler*.

Once you spot these patterns, a large share of French sentences breaks into predictable blocks.

## Meanings that surprise

Several of these verbs have more than one meaning, and the second one often confuses readers.

- *Faire* also appears in weather and time phrases: *il fait froid* (it is cold), *il fait nuit* (it is dark).
- *Arriver* can mean "to happen": *Qu'est-ce qui est arrivé ?* (What happened?)
- *Prendre* appears in many phrases: *prendre un café*, *prendre une décision*, *prendre le temps*.
- *Passer* (not in the list, but just behind it) means both "to pass" and "to spend time".

Treat these as vocabulary in their own right. A verb you know in only one meaning is a verb that will trip you up.

## Irregular forms to recognise

You will not need to produce these straight away. But recognising them saves you from lookups.

| Verb | Forms you will see |
| --- | --- |
| *être* | *suis*, *es*, *est*, *sommes*, *êtes*, *sont*, *était* |
| *avoir* | *ai*, *as*, *a*, *avons*, *avez*, *ont*, *avait* |
| *aller* | *vais*, *vas*, *va*, *allons*, *allez*, *vont* |
| *faire* | *fais*, *fait*, *faisons*, *faites*, *font* |
| *pouvoir* | *peux*, *peut*, *pouvons*, *peuvent* |

## A short passage

Find the verbs and say which of the twenty each comes from.

> *Hier, Marie a pris le train pour Lyon. Elle voulait voir sa sœur, qui est malade. Elle a trouvé un taxi à la gare et elle est arrivée à midi.*

**Answers:** *a pris* (prendre), *voulait* (vouloir), *voir* (voir), *est* (être), *a trouvé* (trouver), *est arrivée* (arriver).

## How to use this list

1. **Read the table once**, then cover the English and recall the meaning.
2. **Look for these verbs** in the next text you read, and underline them.
3. **Note forms you did not recognise.** Each unfamiliar form is something to add to your list.
4. **Move on.** After a few weeks, these verbs will be automatic.
