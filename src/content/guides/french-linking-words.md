## Why linking words decide the meaning

When you can read every word of a sentence and still feel lost, the missing piece is often a small connecting word. *Pourtant*, *donc*, *alors que* and *bien que* tell you how one idea relates to the next: opposition, cause, result or time. They are short and easy to skim, and they are some of the most important words in the text.

This guide groups them by function, so you can learn them in families.

## By function

### Adding

| Word | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| *et* | and |
| *aussi* | also |
| *de plus* | moreover, what is more |
| *en outre* | in addition (formal) |

### Contrast

| Word | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *mais* | but | *Il est fatigué, mais il continue.* |
| *pourtant* | yet, even so | *Il pleuvait, pourtant elle est sortie.* |
| *cependant* | however | *Le projet est difficile ; cependant il avance.* |
| *toutefois* | nevertheless (formal) | *Toutefois, rien n'est décidé.* |
| *alors que*, *tandis que* | whereas, while | *Elle aime lire, alors que lui préfère courir.* |

### Cause

| Word | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *parce que* | because | *Je reste parce que je suis fatigué.* |
| *car* | for, because (written) | *Il est parti tôt, car il avait un rendez-vous.* |
| *puisque* | since (a known reason) | *Puisque tu es là, aide-moi.* |
| *à cause de* | because of (usually negative) | *à cause de la pluie* |
| *grâce à* | thanks to | *grâce à son aide* |

### Consequence

| Word | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *donc* | so, therefore | *Il pleut, donc nous restons.* |
| *alors* | so, then | *Il était tard, alors elle est rentrée.* |
| *c'est pourquoi* | that is why | *C'est pourquoi il a refusé.* |
| *par conséquent* | consequently (formal) | *Par conséquent, la réunion est annulée.* |

### Time and order

| Word | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| *d'abord* | first |
| *ensuite*, *puis* | then, next |
| *enfin* | finally |
| *pendant que* | while |
| *lorsque*, *quand* | when |

### Concession

| Word | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *bien que* | although (+ subjunctive) | *Bien qu'il pleuve, nous sortons.* |
| *même si* | even if | *Même s'il pleut, nous sortons.* |
| *malgré* | despite | *malgré la fatigue* |

## The small differences that matter

**Parce que, car, puisque.** All three mean "because", but they behave differently. *Parce que* gives the real cause and answers "why?". *Car* adds an explanation and sounds more written. *Puisque* introduces a reason that you can take for granted: *Puisque tu es là, aide-moi* ("Since you're here, help me").

**À cause de and grâce à.** Both mean "because of", but *à cause de* usually points to something bad and *grâce à* to something good. *À cause de la pluie* is a complaint; *grâce à son aide* is a thank-you.

**Bien que and même si.** *Bien que* takes the subjunctive and states a fact that does not stop the action, while *même si* takes the indicative and imagines a hypothetical.

## A worked passage

Read it and name the function of each linking word.

> *D'abord, il a hésité, parce qu'il avait peur. Pourtant, il a fini par accepter, puisque son ami insistait. Bien qu'il soit encore inquiet, il a signé le contrat ; c'est pourquoi il se sent plus calme aujourd'hui.*

- *d'abord*: order.
- *parce que*: cause.
- *pourtant*: contrast.
- *puisque*: known reason.
- *bien que*: concession (followed by the subjunctive *soit*).
- *c'est pourquoi*: consequence.

## How to practise

1. **Circle them.** In one paragraph, circle every linking word.
2. **Name the function.** Label each one: contrast, cause, consequence, time.
3. **Predict.** Before you read past a linking word, guess what kind of idea comes next.
4. **Reread.** If the paragraph did not make sense, reread it with the links in mind.
