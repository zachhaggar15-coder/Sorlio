## Two kinds of French reading

French reading material falls into two broad groups.

- **Graded texts** are written or adapted for learners. The vocabulary and grammar are controlled to a level, and they often come with notes, glossaries or audio.
- **Authentic texts** are written for native readers: novels, newspapers, websites, subtitles. There is no adjustment for the learner.

Both are useful. The skill is knowing which to read when.

## What graded texts do well

- **You can understand them.** That is the main advantage. When most of the page is readable, you can read for meaning and learn words from context.
- **They are predictable.** The same vocabulary returns, so you meet words more than once.
- **They give you a clear ladder.** Levels from A1 upward let you move step by step.
- **They build confidence.** Finishing a story in French is motivating.

Their weakness is artificiality. Graded text can feel flat, and it does not teach you the full range of idiom, register and speed you will meet in the wild.

## What authentic texts do well

- **They are real.** You meet French as people actually write it, with its irregularities.
- **They are varied.** Topics, tones and styles are broader than any graded series.
- **They match your interests.** You can read what you actually care about.
- **They teach you the gap.** You see exactly what you do not know yet.

Their weakness is difficulty. A page with too many unknown words is a frustrating experience and a poor way to learn.

## A comparison

| | Graded texts | Authentic texts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Difficulty | Controlled | Uncontrolled |
| Vocabulary | Repeated, limited | Wide, varied |
| Support | Glossaries, audio, exercises | Usually none |
| Motivation | Easy wins | Rewarding but harder |
| Best for | A1 to B1, building the habit | B1 and above, widening range |

## Signs you are ready to switch

Look for these signals:

1. **You read a graded text at your level with few lookups,** and it starts to feel too easy.
2. **You find the language predictable** and notice you are skimming.
3. **You can follow the main idea of an authentic paragraph,** even if you miss some words.
4. **You want to read about things graded texts do not cover.**

You do not have to switch completely. Many learners keep a graded text going while reading authentic material in short bursts.

## Bridge material

There is a stage between the two, and it helps to name it. Some authentic material is easier than other authentic material.

- **Children's books and young-adult fiction** use plain language and clear plots.
- **Comics and graphic novels** support the text with pictures.
- **News written for younger readers or for learners** is real but simplified in tone.
- **Subtitled video with French subtitles** gives you audio and text together.
- **Short contemporary fiction** with dialogue is easier than long descriptive writing.

Start your authentic reading here, and move toward adult fiction and newspapers as your range grows.

## A transition plan

| Stage | What to read | Proportion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before the switch | Graded texts only | All |
| First month | Graded texts plus one bridge text a week | About 80 percent graded |
| Second month | Graded and bridge texts equally | About 50 percent |
| Third month | Authentic texts mostly, graded for rest | About 20 percent graded |

Treat these numbers as a rough guide. Adjust to how you feel, not to a schedule.

## What to keep from graded reading

When you move on, keep the habits that made graded reading work:

- Read at a level where you know most of the words.
- Track the words that return.
- Review a small list regularly.

The material changes, but the method does not.
