## About this practice text

This is an original reading passage written for this guide. It is not taken from a published book or article. It aims at the A2 level of the CEFR, which means short sentences, common everyday vocabulary and the present, the *passé composé* and the near future.

We wrote it ourselves and checked the grammar, but it has not been reviewed by a native-speaker teacher. If you are preparing for an exam, use it alongside a course or a teacher.

## Introduction in English

Claire lives in a small French town. On Sunday morning she goes to the market with her bicycle. She wants tomatoes and apples, and she meets a friend at a café afterwards. In the passage she buys a basket because she forgot her bag at home, and she talks about her holiday plans. Read the passage once for the story, then go back to the vocabulary and grammar sections.

## The passage

**Le marché du dimanche**

Le dimanche matin, Claire se lève tôt. Elle prend son vélo et va au marché de la place Saint-Louis. Il fait beau, mais il y a beaucoup de monde. Elle cherche des tomates et des pommes.

Au stand de Monsieur Durand, elle parle avec le marchand. « Bonjour Claire ! Qu'est-ce que tu veux aujourd'hui ? » demande-t-il. « Je voudrais deux kilos de tomates, s'il vous plaît », répond-elle.

Ce matin, elle a oublié son sac à la maison. Alors elle achète un grand panier en osier. Il coûte cinq euros. Ensuite, elle s'arrête au café pour prendre un café au lait avec son amie Sophie. Sophie habite à côté du marché depuis deux ans.

Elles parlent de leurs vacances. Claire va partir en Bretagne le mois prochain. Elle va prendre le train et rester une semaine chez sa sœur.

## Grammar in the passage

**The passé composé.** *Elle a oublié son sac* means "she forgot her bag". It is formed with *avoir* plus the past participle *oublié*. Use it for a completed action in the past.

**The near future (futur proche).** *Claire va partir en Bretagne* means "Claire is going to leave for Brittany". Form it with *aller* plus an infinitive. It is the everyday way to talk about plans.

**Depuis with the present.** *Sophie habite à côté du marché depuis deux ans* means "Sophie has lived next to the market for two years". Even though the English uses the perfect, French uses the present with *depuis* for an action that continues now.

**Polite conditional.** *Je voudrais deux kilos* means "I would like two kilos". *Voudrais* is the conditional of *vouloir*, and it is more polite than *je veux*.

**Reflexive verbs.** *Elle se lève*, *elle s'arrête* and *se lever* use a reflexive pronoun. Learn them as verbs with the pronoun attached.

## Comprehension questions

1. When does Claire get up on Sunday morning?
2. How does she travel to the market?
3. What does she want to buy at Monsieur Durand's stand?
4. Why does she buy a basket?
5. How much does the basket cost?
6. Who does Claire meet at the café?
7. How long has Sophie lived near the market?
8. Where is Claire going on holiday, and when?
9. How will she travel there?
10. Who will she stay with?

## Answers and explanations

1. She gets up early (*tôt*).
2. She goes by bicycle (*son vélo*).
3. Tomatoes. She asks for two kilos.
4. She forgot her bag at home this morning (*ce matin*), so she needs something to carry her shopping.
5. Five euros.
6. Her friend Sophie.
7. For two years (*depuis deux ans*).
8. Brittany, next month (*le mois prochain*).
9. By train (*le train*).
10. Her sister (*sa sœur*).

## Further practice

- **Retell the passage in your own words** in the present tense, then in the past tense. This practises the *passé composé*.
- **Write three sentences about your own plans** using *aller* plus an infinitive.
- **Read the passage aloud** and record yourself. Compare it with a native recording if you can find one.
- **Find five verbs** you know and write the present, *passé composé* and near future for each.
- **Keep a list** of words you looked up, and review it after two days.
