## An unfair advantage

English borrowed a very large part of its vocabulary from French, especially after 1066 and in formal, legal, scientific and cultural vocabulary. That means an English reader walks into a French text already knowing many words, if they learn to see them. The trick is that the spelling changes in regular ways. Learn the patterns, and you can decode words you have never studied.

This guide shows the patterns that give the most return for the least effort.

## Patterns that work

| French ending or start | English equivalent | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *-tion* | *-tion* | *nation*, *information*, *éducation* |
| *-ité* | *-ity* | *université* (university), *qualité* (quality), *réalité* (reality) |
| *-ment* (adverbs) | *-ly* | *rapidement* (rapidly), *finalement* (finally), *probablement* (probably) |
| *-eux*, *-euse* | *-ous* | *dangereux* (dangerous), *sérieux* (serious), *nerveux* (nervous) |
| *-ique* | *-ic*, *-ical* | *politique* (political), *économique* (economic), *magique* (magic) |
| *-oire* | *-ory* | *victoire* (victory), *mémoire* (memory), *gloire* (glory) |
| *-aire* | *-ary* | *ordinaire* (ordinary), *secrétaire* (secretary), *anniversaire* (anniversary) |
| *-ant*, *-ent* | *-ing*, *-ent* | *intéressant* (interesting), *différent* (different) |
| *é-* | *s-* | *école* (school), *étudiant* (student), *état* (state), *étrange* (strange) |

## The hat that means a missing s

The circumflex (*^*) often marks a lost *s*, which English kept:

| French | English |
| --- | --- |
| *forêt* | forest |
| *hôpital* | hospital |
| *île* | isle |
| *hôte* | host |
| *côte* | coast |
| *fête* | feast |

When you see a circumflex, try putting an *s* back after the vowel and see whether an English word appears.

## A worked passage

Read this and see how many words you can recognise without help.

> *L'université a organisé une conférence politique sur la qualité de l'éducation. Les étudiants ont écouté attentivement le discours sérieux du directeur.*

You can probably guess: *université* (university), *organisé* (organised), *conférence* (conference), *politique* (political), *qualité* (quality), *éducation* (education), *étudiants* (students), *attentivement* (attentively), *sérieux* (serious), *directeur* (director). Almost the whole passage opens up before you have looked anything up.

## Where cognates mislead

The pattern is strong, but it is not perfect. A few words look right and are wrong:

- *Actuellement* is "currently", not "actually".
- *Librairie* is a bookshop, not a library.
- *Éventuellement* is "possibly", not "eventually".

When a cognate gives you a sentence that does not make sense, stop and check. Our [false friends guide](/french-false-friends/) lists the common traps.

## Making it a habit

1. **Look for the ending first.** Before you decode a word, glance at the last syllables. Many words fall into a pattern.
2. **Say the word aloud.** French spelling is different, but the sound is often closer to English than the spelling suggests.
3. **Collect your own families.** When you notice a pattern, write down three examples. They become your quick-recognition kit.
4. **Do not stop to confirm.** In extensive reading, accept an obvious cognate and keep going.

## A short exercise

Guess the English meaning, then check.

1. *nationalité*
2. *rapidement*
3. *dangereux*
4. *hôpital*
5. *victoire*
6. *anniversaire*

**Answers:** 1. nationality. 2. rapidly, quickly. 3. dangerous. 4. hospital. 5. victory. 6. anniversary (or birthday).
