## The short verdict

Duolingo and Sorlio solve different problems. Duolingo is built around short, structured lessons that help you start and keep a daily habit. Sorlio is built around reading French stories and news, with vocabulary learned in context. If you are a beginner who wants a guided start, Duolingo is a sensible choice. If you can already read a little and want to read more, Sorlio is designed for that next step. Many learners use both.

## Teaching approach

Duolingo teaches by sequence. Each unit introduces a small set of words and structures, then asks you to use them in different exercises. The design rewards consistency. It is effective at getting you through the basics and keeping you returning.

Sorlio teaches by exposure. You read a story or news item at a level you choose, look up words you do not know, and save them. The learning happens in the sentences you are reading. That suits learners who find lesson-style content too controlled, but it asks more of you because you choose what to read.

## Reading practice

Duolingo includes reading within its exercises. These are usually short sentences, chosen to practise a specific point. They are good for recognition and for building up the first few hundred words.

Sorlio is built around longer reading. Full stories and news items let you practise following a text from beginning to end, which is the skill you need for books and articles. The difficulty depends on the content you choose, so start below your current level if you are unsure.

## Vocabulary learning

Duolingo introduces vocabulary in lessons and reviews it through exercises. The words you learn are the ones in the course.

In Sorlio you learn vocabulary from the text you are reading. A word that appears in a sentence about your own interests is more likely to stick, and the saved-word review lets you return to the words you looked up. This works best once you have enough base vocabulary to read the surrounding sentence.

## Listening

Duolingo includes audio in its exercises, which helps with pronunciation and listening to isolated sentences. Sorlio offers listening while reading, which gives you spoken French alongside text. Neither product is a substitute for conversation practice.

## Grammar

Duolingo introduces grammar through lessons and exercises that show the rule in use. This is one of its strengths for beginners.

Sorlio includes grammar lessons and comprehension checks, and non-AI practice is free. Grammar there supports your reading rather than driving the whole course, so if explanations are central to how you learn, you may want a dedicated grammar reference or course alongside any app.

## Free features and subscriptions

Both products offer a free entry point, and the details differ.

For Sorlio, reading, news, importing your own texts, listening, dictionary lookups, grammar and non-AI practice are free, and no account is needed. The one limit you will notice is 5 new saved words a day, though you can review the words you have saved without limit. Premium adds unlimited word saving, AI explanations of words and sentences, AI translations of news and imported texts, and AI paraphrase practice. It is a monthly subscription billed through Google Play, it needs an account, and there is no free trial. The price is shown in Google Play before you buy and can vary by country.

For Duolingo, the scope of free access and the paid tiers change over time, and we have not confirmed the current terms, so check its official site before you subscribe.

## Long-term study experience

Duolingo is a good habit tool. Its structure and streaks help many people return every day, and it gives a clear sense of progress through a course.

Sorlio is a reading habit tool. Over time you build a personal set of saved words and a sense of how much more French you can now read. The experience is less guided, so it works best for learners who are already motivated to read.

## Where Sorlio is weaker

A fair comparison should say where our product falls short:

- **Android only.** If you use an iPhone, Sorlio is not an option today.
- **Less guidance.** There is no single fixed course that walks you from zero to a level. You choose what to read.
- **No speaking practice.** It trains reading, vocabulary and listening, not speaking or pronunciation feedback.
- **Device voice, not recordings.** Listening uses your phone's text-to-speech voice, which is not the same as recorded native speakers.
- **News is formal.** It comes from official French and EU public sources, so it is authentic but not written for learners.

## Which should you choose?

- **Choose Duolingo** if you are a beginner, want a guided path, and value a short daily routine.
- **Choose Sorlio** if you can read simple French, want to read stories and news, and want to learn vocabulary in context on Android.
- **Use both** if you want the structure of a course for the basics and reading practice for comprehension. A common pattern is a short Duolingo session each day and a reading session two or three times a week.

## Using both effectively

A workable routine is simple. Keep your daily lesson, because consistency matters. Once a week or more, read a short Sorlio story at a level just below your comfort zone. Look up the words you need, save the ones that appear often, and review them. When your reading becomes easier, move up a level or choose longer texts. Let your reading show you where your gaps are, and use the course to fill them.
