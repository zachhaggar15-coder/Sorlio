## What it is, and why it frightens learners

The subjunctive is a verb form used after certain expressions: things you want, fear, doubt, or need to happen, and some conjunctions. English uses it rarely ("I suggest that he be present"), so it feels exotic. French uses it all the time, which is why learners often meet it by B1.

The reassuring point for readers: you do not have to build the subjunctive. You only have to notice it, and see that it comes with a trigger.

## Start with the triggers

The subjunctive almost always follows *que* after a trigger phrase. Learn to recognise the trigger, and the verb that follows is a signal.

| Group | Triggers | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Need | *il faut que*, *il est nécessaire que* | *Il faut que tu partes.* (You must leave.) |
| Want | *vouloir que*, *souhaiter que* | *Je veux que tu viennes.* (I want you to come.) |
| Feeling | *être content que*, *avoir peur que*, *regretter que* | *Je suis content que tu sois là.* (I'm glad you're here.) |
| Doubt | *douter que*, *ne pas penser que* | *Je ne pense pas qu'il vienne.* (I don't think he's coming.) |
| Conjunctions | *bien que*, *pour que*, *avant que*, *à condition que* | *Bien qu'il pleuve, nous sortons.* (Although it's raining, we go out.) |

## What the forms look like

For most regular verbs, the present subjunctive looks like the present indicative with a few differences.

- *que je parle*, *que tu parles*, *qu'il parle*, *que nous parlions*, *que vous parliez*, *qu'ils parlent*

The *-ions* and *-iez* endings are the most useful to notice, because they appear in the *nous* and *vous* forms and look different from the indicative.

Several common verbs are irregular. You will meet these so often that it helps to recognise them:

| Verb | Subjunctive form (il / elle) | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *être* | *soit* | *Il faut qu'elle soit prête.* |
| *avoir* | *ait* | *Je doute qu'il ait raison.* |
| *aller* | *aille* | *Je veux qu'il aille chez le médecin.* |
| *faire* | *fasse* | *Il faut que tu fasses attention.* |
| *pouvoir* | *puisse* | *Bien qu'il puisse venir, il hésite.* |
| *savoir* | *sache* | *Je veux qu'il sache la vérité.* |

## The pairs that teach you the contrast

The same verb of thinking can flip tense depending on whether it is affirmative or negative:

> *Je pense qu'il vient.* (indicative: I think he is coming.)
> *Je ne pense pas qu'il vienne.* (subjunctive: I don't think he is coming.)

The first states a belief, so it uses the indicative. The second expresses doubt, so it uses the subjunctive. Reading these side by side is one of the best ways to feel the difference.

## Recognising it in a text

> *Bien que le projet ait été retardé, il faut que nous finissions avant la fin du mois.*

- *Bien que… ait été*: the conjunction *bien que* triggers the subjunctive.
- *il faut que nous finissions*: a need phrase followed by the *nous* form with *-ions*.

You do not have to translate every word to see the structure. Spot the triggers, and you know the sentence is about a concession and a requirement.

## A short exercise

Underline the trigger and say why it needs the subjunctive, then check.

1. *Il faut que tu fasses tes devoirs.*
2. *Je suis content que vous soyez venus.*
3. *Elle part avant que la pluie commence.*
4. *Je sais qu'il est malade.*

**Answers:** 1. *Il faut que*, a need, followed by *fasses*. 2. *Content que*, a feeling, followed by *soyez*. 3. *Avant que*, a conjunction, followed by *commence*. 4. No subjunctive: *je sais que* states a fact, so *est* is indicative.

## How to practise

Each time you read *que* in a French text, glance at the words before it. If they express need, wish, feeling, doubt or one of the conjunctions above, label the verb as subjunctive in your head. Do this for a week and the pattern becomes automatic.
