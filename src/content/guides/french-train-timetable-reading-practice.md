## Read a timetable in French

A timetable is a useful reading challenge because you need to combine times, destinations and small changes in a notice. This fictional example is an original exercise, not a real service. It is intended as roughly A2 practice rather than an official level assessment. Read the table first; then use the notice to check whether the normal information has changed.

| Train | Départ de Tours | Arrivée à Blois | Quai |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | 08 h 15 | 08 h 52 | 2 |
| B | 09 h 10 | 09 h 47 | 4 |
| C | 10 h 05 | 10 h 42 | 1 |

**Avis aux voyageurs :** Le train B à destination de Blois partira avec dix minutes de retard. Aujourd'hui, il partira du quai 3 et non du quai 4. Les autres trains circulent normalement.

## Questions

1. Which train normally leaves at 09:10?
2. How long is its delay, and what is its new departure time if the stated delay does not change?
3. Which platform should its passengers use today?
4. Does the notice say that train C is delayed?
5. How long is the scheduled journey on train A?

Try to answer without translating every word. Times and train letters are anchors; the notice tells you which entry they affect.

## Answers and reasoning

1. Train B. Find **09 h 10** in the departure column, then read across.
2. Ten minutes. That suggests 09:20, although a real station may update its estimates.
3. Platform 3. *Du quai 3 et non du quai 4* replaces the platform shown in the normal table.
4. No. The final sentence says the other trains are running normally. It does not promise there will never be a later change.
5. Thirty-seven minutes, from 08:15 to 08:52.

## Words and patterns to notice

- **départ / arrivée**: departure / arrival. The same word may label a column rather than a full sentence.
- **à destination de**: bound for. It identifies where the train is going.
- **en retard / avec dix minutes de retard**: delayed / ten minutes late.
- **quai**: platform. A *voie* is a track; stations may use either word in different contexts.
- **partira**: will leave, the future form of *partir*. In a real notice the future can signal a change that has not yet happened.
- **et non**: and not. This contrast matters: a familiar number after it is the rejected option.

## A second pass

Cover the English and explain the notice in one sentence. Then change the delay to *vingt minutes* and work out the new estimated departure: 09:30. Finally, write a short notice of your own: *Le train A partira du quai 5.* Compare your sentence with the original and identify the train and platform.

In real travel, check the current information at the station or with the operator: a practice table cannot tell you about live disruption. For another everyday text, try the [A2 story and questions](/a2-french-reading-practice/). If this is comfortable, the [B1 reading exercise](/b1-french-reading-exercises/) adds longer sentences and a decision to interpret.

One final check: say the departure and arrival times aloud in French. This connects the written numbers with the spoken information you might hear at a station.
